**What to take from Portigal:** the guide is a safety net, not a script. The best material comes from following what the participant said, not from getting through your questions. And when they stop talking, wait. The thing they say after the pause is usually the real answer.

→ **Commit** `students/UX{n}/week6/session1-learning.md`

## DO — 2 hours

### R4.1 — Interview 1

**Method**
1. Read your consent script out loud, do not paraphrase it. Get consent recorded.
2. Run the interview. 45 minutes.
3. Record it, with consent. Audio is enough.
4. Write up notes **straight away**. Not tomorrow. You lose the texture within hours.
5. Note format: what they said (quoted where it matters) · what they did · what surprised you · what you now doubt.
6. Anonymise as you write. P1, not a name.

**The one rule for your first interview:** when they stop talking, count to five before you say anything. You will find this excruciating. Do it anyway.

**Done when**
- [ ] Consent obtained and logged
- [ ] 45-minute interview run and recorded
- [ ] Notes written straight away, in the four-part format
- [ ] Quotes are actual quotes, marked as such
- [ ] Fully anonymised
- [ ] "What surprised me" is filled in and not empty

→ `students/UX{n}/week6/R4-1-interview-1.md`

## DO — 2 hours

### R4.2 — Self-critique

**Method**
1. Transcribe at least 10 minutes of interview 1 verbatim. Including your own words. Including "um".
2. Mark every mistake you made:
   - **L** — leading question
   - **Y** — yes/no question
   - **F** — filled a silence
   - **P** — pitched or explained your own idea
   - **M** — missed a follow-up that was clearly there
3. Count each type.
4. Write your three fixes for interview 2, as concrete behaviours.

**Worked example** — a marked fragment:

> **Me:** So was that part confusing? **[L, Y]**
> **P1:** Um, yeah, I guess a bit.
> **Me:** Right, because a lot of people say the wording there is unclear— **[P]**
> **P1:** Actually it wasn't the wording, I just didn't have the—
> **Me:** The document? **[F, and I finished her sentence]**
> **P1:** ...yeah.
>
> **What I lost:** she was about to tell me what she did not have, and it may not have been a document. I put a word in her mouth and I will never know what she was going to say.
>
> **Counts:** L 4 · Y 6 · F 7 · P 2 · M 3
> **Fixes for interview 2:** 1) After every answer, count to five before speaking. 2) Never complete a sentence. 3) Replace "was it confusing" with "walk me through what you did".

**Done when**
- [ ] 10+ minutes transcribed verbatim, including your own words
- [ ] Every instance of all five mistake types marked
- [ ] Counts given
- [ ] At least one place named where you lost information, with what you lost
- [ ] Three fixes stated as behaviours, not intentions

→ `students/UX{n}/week6/R4-2-self-critique.md`

### R4.3 — Interview 2
Same protocol. Apply your three fixes. At the end of the write-up, note whether each fix held.

### R4.6 — First pass at patterns

**Method**
1. Across all five, find things that recurred.
2. For each pattern, state **how many of the five** it appeared in. Always give the denominator. "Users found it confusing" is not a finding; "4 of 5 could not tell which income certificate was required" is.
3. Separate: what they said · what they did · what you infer. Three columns, kept apart.
4. Mark anything that appeared in only one interview as a **single instance**, not a pattern. It may still be the most important thing you heard, and it is still n=1.

**Done when**
- [ ] Every pattern has its denominator out of 5
- [ ] Said, did, and inferred are in separate columns
- [ ] Single instances are labelled as such
- [ ] At least one thing that contradicts what you expected in week 5

→ `students/UX{n}/week6/R4-6-patterns.md`

## DO — 2 hours

### R4.7 — Affinity map

**Method**
1. One observation per note. Not one participant per note.
2. Group by what the observation is *about*, not by which participant said it.
3. Name each group with a sentence, not a label. "Documents" is a label and tells you nothing. "Applicants do not know which of the four income certificates counts" is a finding.
4. Count group sizes and keep the denominators.
5. Photograph or export it and commit it.

**Done when**
- [ ] One observation per note
- [ ] Grouped by topic, not by person
- [ ] Every group named with a full sentence
- [ ] Sizes and denominators recorded
- [ ] Committed as an image plus a written list of the groups

→ `students/UX{n}/week6/R4-7-affinity-map.md`

### P6.1 — Revised hypothesis

**Method**
1. Rewrite your problem statement using what five people actually told you.
2. State your confidence: high, medium, or low, and why.
3. State what you still do not know.
4. **If the brief's original claim survived, say so.** Finding that the client was right is a real finding, and pretending otherwise to look clever is a failure mode the defense will catch.

**Worked example** — the confidence section:

> **Confidence: medium.** 4 of 5 participants stopped at the income certificate step, and 3 of those 4 described not knowing which certificate was valid. That is a consistent signal from a small sample recruited through one channel, which means it may over-represent people who found the process hard enough to talk about. I would raise this to high with drop-off analytics by field, which would take one request to the mission's IT team.

**Done when**
- [ ] Rewritten statement grounded in what participants said or did
- [ ] Confidence level stated with a reason for that level
- [ ] What you still do not know, listed
- [ ] The original claim addressed directly, confirmed or refuted

→ `students/UX{n}/week6/P6-1-revised-hypothesis.md`
