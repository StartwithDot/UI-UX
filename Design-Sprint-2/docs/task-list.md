# Sprint 2 Task List

Weeks 5 to 8. Track letters as in Sprint 1: **C** Craft, **R** Research, **P** Product, **S** Systems, **F** Spine, **X** Injection.

No screens exist until week 7. If you open Figma in week 5, you have misunderstood the sprint.

---

## Week 5: Plan

**P5.1** The client's brief contains a diagnosis and a prescription. Separate them. Write what the client has actually observed, what they have inferred, and what they have prescribed.
→ `students/UX{n}/week5/P5-1-brief-decomposition.md`

**P5.2** List every assumption in the brief and every assumption of your own. Minimum 12. For each: how would you know if it were false, and what it would cost to find out.
→ `P5-2-assumptions.md`

**P5.3** Rank the assumptions by risk: how likely it is wrong multiplied by how much damage it does if it is wrong. The top three are what the research is for.
→ `P5-3-assumption-ranking.md`

**R5.1** Research plan. The question, the method, why that method and not the other three, who you will talk to, what you expect to find, and what result would falsify your expectation.
→ `R5-1-research-plan.md`

**R5.2** Discussion guide. Opening, warm up, the core sequence, and the close. Every question non leading. Include the five questions you drafted in leading form and their corrections, because that edit is the skill.
→ `R5-2-discussion-guide.md`

**R5.3** Consent form and process, per `research-ethics.md`. What you record, where it is stored, how long you keep it, how someone withdraws.
→ `R5-3-consent.md`

**R5.4** Recruit and run two interviews. Transcribe both. Do not synthesise yet.
→ `R5-4-interviews-1-2.md`

**R5.5** After the first interview, write what you did wrong in it. Everyone leads, interrupts, or fills a silence in their first one. Naming it is how the second one improves.
→ `R5-5-interview-self-critique.md`

**F5.1** Why session: cognition, memory, attention, cognitive load. Source: 100 Things Every Designer Needs to Know About People.
→ `fundamentals/why-sessions/week05-cognition.md`

**F5.2** Teardown: Passport Seva appointment booking.
→ `fundamentals/teardowns/05-passport-seva.md`

## Week 6: Collect

**R6.1** Three more interviews. Five total, minimum. Composition must cover at least three of the five segments in the brief.
→ `R6-1-interviews-3-5.md`

**R6.2** Tag every transcript. Build the tag structure before you tag the second one, and note where the structure had to change. Tag changes mid analysis are normal, hiding them is not.
→ `R6-2-tagging.md`

**R6.3** Usability test on the live enrolment portal, 5 participants, three tasks. Record task success, time, drop off point, and every place someone re-read a field label.
→ `R6-3-usability-test.md`

**R6.4** Where in the form do people stop, and why. This is the 38 percent the client called motivation. Answer it with evidence.
→ `R6-4-dropoff-analysis.md`

**R6.5** First synthesis pass. Group observations into candidate findings. Mark each candidate with how many participants support it.
→ `R6-5-first-synthesis.md`

**R6.6** The interview that contradicted your expectation. Write it up separately. If none did, either your questions were leading or you only talked to people who agree with you, and say which.
→ `R6-6-contradiction.md`

**F6.1** Why session: mental models, affordances, signifiers, part two, applied to a form nobody can finish.
→ `fundamentals/why-sessions/week06-mental-models.md`

**F6.2** Teardown: a hospital appointment app.
→ `fundamentals/teardowns/06-hospital-appointment.md`

## Week 7: Synthesise

**R7.1** Findings document. Every finding carries: the statement, the evidence, participant IDs, confidence level, the alternative explanation you considered and rejected, and what would change your mind.
→ `R7-1-findings.md`

**R7.2** Card sort with 8 participants on the scheme's content, then a tree test on the resulting structure. Report both, including where the tree test contradicted the card sort.
→ `R7-2-ia-validation.md`

**R7.3** Take your riskiest remaining assumption and design a probe for it. Not an interview question, a designed artefact that produces a behaviour you can observe.
→ `R7-3-assumption-probe.md`

**R7.4** Opportunity solution tree: the outcome at the top, the opportunities found in research, the solutions considered, the experiment for the one you chose.
→ `R7-4-opportunity-tree.md`

**R7.5** The research repository. Structured so another designer can find the evidence behind any finding in under two minutes.
→ `delivery/research/repository-README.md` plus your tagged transcripts

**P7.1** Was the client right. Answer directly, in writing, with the evidence. If motivation is a real factor, say so and size it. If it is not, say what is and size that.
→ `P7-1-was-the-client-right.md`

**F7.1** Why session: behavioural economics in interfaces. Defaults, anchoring, framing, friction.
→ `fundamentals/why-sessions/week07-behavioural.md`

**F7.2** Teardown: the income tax e-filing portal.
→ `fundamentals/teardowns/07-income-tax.md`

**F7.3** Whiteboard why. Reconstruct your findings from memory, with confidence levels. 15 minutes.

**C8.1** Redesign the enrolment flow to answer the problem you found, not the one you were given. Mid to high fidelity, on the cohort's design system.
→ `C8-1-enrolment-redesign.md` plus Figma link

→ `C8-2-finding-to-decision-map.md`

**C8.3** The 3 to 11 week wait cannot be designed away. Design the communication across it: what is sent, when, through which channel, and what it says.

**C8.4** All states, gates run, same standard as Sprint 1.

**P8.2** The app question. The client asked for an app with gamification. Answer it with evidence, either way. "No" needs as much evidence as "yes".

**S8.1** Any component the research demanded, contributed to the shared system with the finding that justified it.

**F8.2** Teardown: a UPI payment app confirmation flow.

## What fails review in this sprint

| Phrase | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Users found it confusing" | No participant, no task, no behaviour |
| "Research shows" with no citation | Nothing shown |
| "Most participants" with n of 5 | Three of five is three of five, say it |
| "This validates our hypothesis" | Research does not validate, it informs. The word choice reveals the intent. |
| A finding with no confidence level | Unweighted evidence is unusable by anyone else |
| A finding that exactly matches what you expected in week 5, with no contradiction anywhere | Either you got lucky or you led every interview |
