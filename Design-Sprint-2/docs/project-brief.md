# Sprint 2 Project Brief: The Skill Scheme Dropout

**Weeks 5 to 8 | Track focus: Research and Evidence | Cohort: UX1 to UX5**

---

## 1. The client story

A state skill development mission runs free vocational training. Enrolment is online, training is at a partner centre, and completion carries a certificate that partner employers recognise.

The numbers they have:

| Stage | Count per 1000 |
|---|---|
| Started the online enrolment | 1000 |
| Completed enrolment | 620 |
| Attended the first class | 250 |
| Completed the course | 180 |
| Received the certificate | 165 |

Their brief: "the dropout is a motivation problem, so we want a mobile app with reminders and gamification".

That brief contains a diagnosis and a prescription, and neither is evidenced. Your first job is to establish whether the diagnosis is true. It may be. It probably is not, because a 38 percent drop inside a form is not usually motivation.

## 2. Why this project

Sprint 1 gave you a flow with a visible problem. This one gives you a number and no explanation. That gap is where research actually lives.

There is also a specific trap built in: the client has already told you the answer and asked for a solution. Accepting it is the easiest way to fail this sprint, and it is also the most common thing that happens in real work.

## 3. What is known and what is not

**Known**
- The funnel numbers above, from their own analytics, collected over 18 months
- Enrolment requires: Aadhaar number, education certificate upload, bank account details, category certificate if applicable
- Course allocation is by centre capacity, not by preference
- The gap between enrolment and first class is between 3 and 11 weeks
- 70 percent of enrolments come from mobile

**Not known, and worth listing because this is the actual scope**
- Where inside the enrolment form people stop
- Whether the people who enrol and never attend received any communication
- Whether the allocated centre was reachable for them
- Whether the course allocated was the course they wanted
- Whether they had already found work by the time the class started
- Whether the certificate is worth anything to the employers who recognise it

That last one is the uncomfortable question, and a designer who asks it has understood what research is for.

## 4. Who to talk to

Five interviews per designer, minimum. Composition matters more than count.

| Segment | Why | How to find them |
|---|---|---|
| Enrolled, attended, completed | The success case, and the least informative | Partner centre |
| Enrolled, attended, dropped out | Where the training itself fails | Partner centre |
| Enrolled, never attended | **The largest group and the one nobody has spoken to** | Centre records, or snowball from the above |
| Started enrolment, never finished | The form problem, if there is one | Hardest to reach. Anyone who has abandoned any government form counts as an analogue. |
| Centre coordinator | The operational reality | Direct approach |

**The reachability problem is real and it is part of the exercise.** You will not easily find people who abandoned a form 8 months ago. State what you could not reach and what that does to your confidence. A findings document that admits its sampling bias is stronger than one that pretends it does not have one.

## 5. Constraints

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Enrolment fields are mandated by the funding rules | You cannot remove fields. You can reorder, explain, defer, and split. |
| Centre allocation is by capacity | Preference matching is out of scope unless you can show the cost of not having it |
| The 3 to 11 week gap is operational, not fixable by design | Everything about that gap is expectation setting |
| No budget for an app | The client asked for one. Your answer must address why or why not. |
| Participants are real people with real time | Consent, compensation where possible, and no extraction |

## 6. Success measures

**Primary:** first class attendance rate among those who complete enrolment. Currently 250 of 620, which is 40 percent.

**Input metrics:** enrolment form completion rate, drop off by field, time from enrolment to first contact, proportion who know their centre and date one week before class.

**Guardrail:** completion rate must not fall. Getting more people to the first class by lowering the bar produces a worse outcome for the person who then drops out in week three, having given up something to be there.

## 7. Deliverables by week

| Week | Ships |
|---|---|
| 5 | Assumption list, research plan, discussion guide, ethics and consent set up, 2 interviews done |
| 6 | 3 more interviews, transcripts tagged, usability test on the live enrolment portal with 5 participants, first synthesis |
| 7 | Findings document with confidence levels, card sort and tree test, one assumption probe, opportunity solution tree |
| 8 | Redesigned enrolment flow answering the researched problem, research repository, defense |

## 8. The deliverable that matters most

Not the redesign. The **findings document**.

It must be usable by someone who was not in the room. That means: the finding, the evidence, the participant IDs, the confidence level, the alternative explanation you considered, and what would change your mind.

A findings document that another designer picks up cold and designs from correctly is the artefact this sprint is built around, and it is also the artefact most portfolios never contain.

## 9. What the client will do

In week 7 the mission's director will state, through the core admin, that the research is interesting but the app is already approved and the timeline stands.

Your response to that is part of the sprint. Designers who have evidence can push back with it. Designers who have opinions cannot. That asymmetry is the entire point of these four weeks.
