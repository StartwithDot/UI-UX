# Sprint 2 — Read First

**Weeks 5 to 8 · Research and problem framing**

---

## What changes from Sprint 1

In Sprint 1 you were given a brief and three described users, and you designed from them. In Sprint 2 you are given a brief that is **wrong**, and your job is to find out how.

You will talk to five real people. You will run a usability study. You will produce findings with confidence levels attached, and you will be asked to defend your sample size to someone who wants to dismiss it.

---

## The project

A school scholarship application, for an education mission. Students and their parents apply for a state scholarship. The mission believes the drop-off is caused by the form being long. The evidence for that belief is one meeting.

Your job in week 5 is to work out whether that belief is true, before anyone designs anything.

## What is harder than Sprint 1

**Real people, real scheduling.** Five interviews means five humans who cancel, arrive late, and say things you did not plan for. Recruitment starts in week 5, not week 6, because it always takes longer than you think.

**You cannot hide behind craft.** In Sprint 1 a beautiful screen bought you some goodwill. In Sprint 2 a beautiful screen with no evidence behind it fails the evidence gate.

**Your first interview will be bad.** You will lead the witness, fill silences, and pitch your idea. You will then transcribe it and see yourself doing all three. That is the point of the self-critique task.

**Five is a small number and you must own it.** In the defense you will be told your sample is too small to conclude anything. The correct response is not to defend it as large. It is to say precisely what five people can and cannot tell you.

## What ships at the end of week 8

- A ranked assumption list with the riskiest one named
- A research plan with a stated method, sample, and what would falsify it
- 5 interviews, with consent, notes, and transcripts
- A self-critique of your own interviewing
- A usability study on 5 participants with severity ratings
- Findings with confidence levels, including at least one contradiction you did not resolve
- A finding-to-decision map: every design change traced to evidence
- A redesigned flow based on what you found, not what you assumed
- One post mortem on the mid-sprint failure
- 20 learning summaries
- A passed defense
