# Design Sprint 2, Admin

Private. The general admin machinery is in `../Design-Sprint-1-Admin/`: the defense runbook, the rubrics, the ledger, and the numbers question bank all carry forward unchanged. This file covers only what is specific to Sprint 2.

---

## What this sprint is testing

Whether a designer can hold a position with evidence rather than confidence. Everything else in the sprint is instrumentation for that one question.

## Weekly focus

**Week 5.** Read the assumption lists first, not the research plans. A designer who lists 12 assumptions but ranks none of them by risk will research the comfortable ones. The intervention is one question: "which of these, if wrong, kills the project".

Watch for research plans that cannot fail. If the plan has no falsification condition, they have designed a plan to confirm what they already think.

**Week 6.** Read the self critiques of the first interview before the transcripts. Everyone leads their first interview. A designer who cannot name what they did wrong in it did not notice, and will do it four more times.

The tell for a bad interview, from the transcript: the interviewer's turns are longer than the participant's.

**Week 7.** The injection lands Monday. Two participants withdraw consent. Every finding built on them must come out, and the confidence on everything adjacent drops.

Watch for who quietly leaves the findings in and only removes the quotes. That is the behaviour the injection exists to surface, and it is a dishonesty flag, not a mistake flag.

**Week 8.** The finding to decision map is the highest signal artefact of the sprint. A designer with 14 findings and 3 design responses has either found things that do not matter or is designing from instinct and citing research afterwards. The map makes it visible in one page.

## The injection

`failure-injections/sprint2.md`. Delivered week 7 Monday.

Two participants withdraw. Chosen because it forces the question that separates real research from decorated opinion: was this finding actually traceable to a person, or was it assembled from a general impression and attributed afterwards.

A designer whose findings are properly sourced can execute the removal in an hour and state exactly what confidence dropped. A designer who cannot has been writing conclusions and citing participants as decoration.

## Answers

`answers/week5.md` to `week8.md`, same structure as Sprint 1: the model answer, the acceptable range, and the shape of wrong.

The one to read closely is week 7. The correct answer to "was the client right" is not simply no. Motivation is probably a real factor for some segment. A designer who says "motivation is not the problem, the form is" has overcorrected and is now as unevidenced as the client was.

## Defense notes for this sprint

**Numbers round additions:** their own sample sizes per segment, what n of 5 can and cannot support, the funnel numbers from the brief, and their confidence thresholds.

**The adversarial round to run:** "you spoke to five people out of 1,900 dropouts. Your recommendation reverses a decision the mission made with 18 months of data. Justify that."

The correct answer names what qualitative work can establish that quantitative cannot, states the sampling limitation without being pushed to it, and does not claim more than five conversations can carry. A designer who defends five interviews as statistically representative has misunderstood their own method.

**The reflection round to watch for:** the designer who says their research went well. In a first real research sprint, it did not. Something was led, someone was unreachable, one segment was missed. A reflection with no methodological error named means they have not evaluated their own process.

## The failure specific to this sprint

Designers who fall in love with a finding in week 6 and spend weeks 7 and 8 protecting it. It shows up as a findings document where every finding points the same direction, and a contradiction file that says nothing contradicted.

The intervention, in week 7, before the findings are final: "which of your findings would you be least willing to give up, and why is that."
