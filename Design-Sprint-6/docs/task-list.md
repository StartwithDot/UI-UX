# Sprint 6 Task List

Weeks 21 to 24. **C** Craft, **R** Research, **P** Product, **S** Systems, **F** Spine, **X** Injection.

Alone this time. The cohort reviews your work, it does not build it with you.

---

## Week 21: Discover

**P21.1** Problem chosen, route stated, and for Route A the written agreement from the organisation committed. Scope statement: what will ship in four weeks and what will not.
→ `students/UX{n}/week21/P21-1-capstone-scope.md`

**P21.2** Re-read all of your week 1 work. Write one page on what you can now see in it that you could not see then. Be specific, name the file and the mistake.
→ `P21-2-week1-reread.md`

**P21.3** Baseline. The number as it stands today, its source, and how it is calculated. If no number exists, you have to create the measurement before you design.
→ `P21-3-baseline.md`

**R21.1** Research plan and 5 sessions run. Your own participants, your own recruitment, consent per the Sprint 2 ethics document.
→ `R21-1-research.md`

**R21.2** Findings with confidence levels, participant IDs, and the alternative explanation for each.
→ `R21-2-findings.md`

**R21.3** The finding you did not expect. If there is none, say so and say what that means about your questions.
→ `R21-3-surprise.md`

**F21.1** Why session: your choice of topic, presented to the cohort. This is the last one you lead. Pick the thing you most wish you had understood in week 1.
→ `fundamentals/why-sessions/week21-{topic}.md`

## Week 22: Define and design

**P22.1** Problem statement from your evidence, not from the brief. With the metric it maps to.
→ `P22-1-problem-statement.md`

**P22.2** Opportunity solution tree, with the branch you chose and the reason.
→ `P22-2-opportunity-tree.md`

**P22.3** Metrics: primary, input, guardrail. Defined before any design exists, committed before any screen.
→ `P22-3-metrics.md`

**P22.4** Scope with an appetite. What fits, what is cut, and the ranked reason for each cut.
→ `P22-4-scope.md`

**C22.1** Full flow, mid fidelity, on the cohort's system.
→ `C22-1-flow.md`

**C22.2** States matrix, complete, all six conditions for every screen. No gaps, and any deliberate gap explained.
→ `C22-2-states.md`

**C22.3** Content design. Every string, written, with the reason for the ones that were hard.
→ `C22-3-content.md`

**F22.1** Teardown: something in your own capstone's domain, done as adversarially as you would do a stranger's product.
→ `fundamentals/teardowns/22-{subject}.md`

## Week 23: Build, test, ship

**C23.1** High fidelity, both widths, dark mode if the product needs it.
→ `C23-1-high-fidelity.md`

**C23.2** Prototype with real logic, including at least two failure paths.
→ `C23-2-prototype.md`

**R23.1** Test with 5 users. Task success, hesitations, and what they expected that did not happen.
→ `R23-1-test.md`

**C23.2b** Fix what broke. What you changed, what you did not, reasons for both.
→ `C23-2b-revisions.md`

**C23.3** Gate, full, evidence on every line, including the screen reader pass with announced text written out.
→ `C23-3-gate-result.md`

**S23.1** Handoff spec, complete. Reviewed by another designer playing the engineer, and their three questions recorded with your answers.
→ `S23-1-handoff-spec.md`

**S23.2** Any component contributed to the system, documented, reviewed, merged.
→ `../Design-Sprint-1/system/components/`

**P23.1** Instrumentation implemented or specified: every event, its properties, and the question it answers.
→ `P23-1-instrumentation.md`

**P23.2** Ship it, if Route A. Even partially. Record what actually went live and what did not.
→ `P23-2-ship-record.md`

**X6.1 `[THE LAST INJECTION]`** The result contradicts the hypothesis. Your recommendation: ship anyway, roll back, or hold. With the number that would change your mind.
→ `X6-1-contradiction-recommendation.md`

**F23.1** Whiteboard why, final. The entire capstone from memory: problem, evidence, metric, design, and the decision you are least sure about.

## Week 24: Measure, report, defend

**P24.1 `[THE RESULT]`** What happened. Metric before and after, or the plan and its first data. Whether it is distinguishable from noise. What you would need to be confident. If it did not work, why, and which assumption was wrong.
→ `P24-1-result.md`

**P24.2** Retrospective on the whole 24 weeks. What you were wrong about in week 1. What took longest to learn. What you still cannot do.
→ `P24-2-retrospective.md`

**P24.3** Portfolio case study. Eight sections per the brief, including section 7, what you got wrong.
→ `delivery/case-studies/UX{n}-capstone.md`

**S24.1** The handover. Your capstone documented so the organisation, or the next cohort, can continue it without you.
→ `S24-1-handover.md`

**S24.2** Cohort session, final. The design system's state, its changelog, and the note to whoever inherits it.
→ `../Design-Sprint-1/system/docs/HANDOVER.md`

**F24.1** Explainer, final. The one concept you would want to be asked about in an interview, explained properly.
→ `fundamentals/explainers/UX{n}-final.md`

**F24.2** One page to your week 1 self. What you would tell them, knowing what the 24 weeks cost.
→ `fundamentals/explainers/UX{n}-letter.md`

**F24.3** Final defense. 90 minutes, seven rounds, external panel.

## What fails review in this sprint

| Answer | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| No baseline number | You cannot claim a result against nothing |
| Metrics defined after the design | Then they were chosen to flatter it |
| "It shipped and users love it", no measurement | The claim everyone makes and nobody supports |
| A positive result with a sample too small to distinguish from noise, presented as a win | Statistically illiterate, and the panel will find it |
| A case study with no mistakes | You did not look, and a hiring manager will notice |
| A retrospective that names nothing you were wrong about in week 1 | After 24 weeks, that is not humility, it is not having paid attention |
