# Sprint 6 Project Brief: Portfolio, Interview, Market

**Weeks 21 to 24 | Track focus: Communication and getting hired | Cohort: UX1 to UX5**

---

## 1. What this sprint is

For five sprints you designed products. In this one the product is **you**, and the user is a hiring manager with ninety seconds.

Everything the program taught is examined again, from the outside: research, framing, craft, systems, ethics, accessibility, measurement, and the ability to defend all of it against someone trying to take it apart. The deliverable is the evidence, and the evidence is judged by strangers.

You do not start from nothing. The job-readiness track (`../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md`) has been running since Sprint 1.

## 2. What you start with

| From | You already have |
|---|---|
| JR1, week 4 | Positioning, a platform decision, an assets checklist |
| JR2, week 8 | The Sprint 1 case study, a live skeleton, and an external review |
| JR3, week 12 | A live portfolio with two case studies and **Mock interview 1** |
| JR4, week 16 | The design-system case study, a LinkedIn draft, **Mock interview 2** |
| JR5, week 20 | The capstone draft, a target list of 20, **Mock interview 3** |

Week 21 is therefore selection and editing, not first drafts. If you arrive with nothing, the sprint will be four weeks of catching up and you will not finish.

## 3. Requirements, non negotiable

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Case studies | Three, written to `06-case-study-structure.md`, showing three different skills, each with a failure in it |
| Portfolio | Live, working on a phone, passing its own accessibility and performance checks |
| Résumé and profile | One page; a LinkedIn profile that says the same thing |
| Interview answers | 40 prepared, spoken, and cut; the three worst drilled |
| Whiteboard | Five exercises completed under time |
| Recordings | A critique performance and a portfolio walkthrough |
| Applications | Ten real ones, sent and tracked |
| Outreach | Five specific messages, two public contributions |
| Mock loop | A full loop with scored feedback from someone who has not seen you before |
| Placement-readiness check | Scored by the external panel in the final loop |
| Final defense | 90 minutes, run as an interview |

## 4. The four weeks

**Week 21, Case studies.** Choose three from five, on evidence. Plan and write them. Cut each by a third.

**Week 22, Portfolio.** Build the site, write the résumé and profile, take an external review of your own presentation, and fix everything it finds.

**Week 23, Interview craft.** Prepared answers, five whiteboard exercises, the critique performance, the recorded walkthrough, behavioural and salary questions.

**Week 24, Market.** Target list, ten applications, outreach, the three-hour mock loop, the final defense, and the 90-day plan.

## 5. The honest result requirement

Every claim in your portfolio is examined for honesty.

- A result you measured is reported with its sample and its limits
- A result you did not measure is labelled as an estimate or left out
- A project that went badly is included if it taught you something, with the mistake named
- Nothing generated by an AI tool is presented as research

**A case study with an honestly reported failure scores higher than one with an unmeasured win.** Everyone claims their design worked. Few can show it, and fewer still can say what they got wrong.

## 6. The final defense, 90 minutes

Run as a real design interview by someone who has not been your reviewer.

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio walk | 20 min | Two case studies, with interruptions |
| Whiteboard | 25 min | A fresh prompt, out loud |
| Craft interrogation | 15 min | Numbers: spacing, contrast, type, motion, tokens |
| Accessibility | 10 min | Any criterion, any project, and how you verified it |
| Systems | 10 min | Tokens, components, governance |
| Ethics | 5 min | Your position, and the last time you tested it |
| Reflection | 5 min | What you are still bad at |

The pass condition is not perfection. It is that you can be given a problem, ask the right questions, reason out loud, defend a decision with evidence, change your mind when shown better evidence, and say honestly where your knowledge ends.

## 7. The placement-readiness check

The mock loop and defense are scored by external people against the rubric in `../06-Job-Readiness-Track.md` section 3. Each designer receives one honest outcome: **Ready**, **Ready with a named gap**, or **Not yet**, with a written list of what to do next. See section 5 of that file for what each means.

The program does not promise a job. It promises an honest assessment, and it tracks what happens next (section 6 of the same file).

## 8. The sprint's injection

There is no sealed scenario in this sprint. The week 22 external review is the injection: a stranger will tell you your portfolio is not good enough, and you will have a day to respond. Your response is graded, not just the fix.

## 9. What ends here and what does not

At week 24 the program ends. What continues:

- The design system, which the next cohort inherits along with your governance model
- The fundamentals folders, with 24 weeks of why sessions, teardowns and explainers
- Your ledger, which is what any reference or placement conversation is built from
- Follow-ups at 30, 90 and 180 days: applications, interviews, offers, and what you would change
- The habit, if it took: nothing is done until someone else can build it, and no decision is real until you can defend it to someone who wants it to be wrong

## 10. Reading

Nothing new is assigned. Week 21 begins with real case studies, read critically, and with `06-case-study-structure.md`.
