# Sprint 6 Project Brief: Capstone, End to End, Measured

**Weeks 21 to 24 | Track focus: All five | Cohort: UX1 to UX5**

---

## 1. What this sprint is

Every designer takes one problem from discovery to a measured result, alone, with the cohort as reviewers rather than collaborators.

Everything the program has taught is examined here at once: research, framing, craft, system, business, measurement, and the ability to defend all of it against someone trying to take it apart.

The distinguishing requirement: **the design must be instrumented and the result must be reported honestly, including when the result is that it did not work.**

## 2. Choosing the problem

Two routes.

**Route A, real.** A real problem for a real organisation. A small business, an NGO, a college department, a local service. Real users, real constraints, real deployment. Preferred, because a real deployment produces real numbers and cannot be argued with.

**Route B, assigned.** For anyone who cannot secure a real one. Assigned by the admin in week 20, from a set with defined constraints and a synthetic but honest measurement environment.

**Route A requirements, all four or it is not Route A:**
1. A named contact at the organisation who has agreed in writing
2. Access to at least five real users
3. Something that can actually ship, even partially, inside four weeks
4. A number that exists before you start

**The trap in Route A** is choosing something too large to ship. A designer with a beautiful unshipped four week project has less to defend than one who shipped a single flow and measured it. Scope down until it can ship, then scope down once more.

## 3. Requirements, non negotiable

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Research | Minimum 5 sessions, primary, with your own participants |
| Problem statement | Framed from evidence, not from the brief you were given |
| Metrics | Primary, input, guardrail, all defined before design starts |
| Craft | Full flow, all states, both widths, on the cohort's design system |
| Accessibility | Gate passed with evidence, screen reader pass included |
| System | Any new component contributed, documented, reviewed |
| Handoff | Complete spec, reviewed by another designer as the engineer |
| Instrumentation | Events defined, and measured if the project shipped |
| Result | Reported honestly, including a null or negative result |
| Defense | 90 minutes, extended, including a panel round |

## 4. The four weeks

**Week 21, Discover.** Problem chosen and agreed, research plan, 5 sessions run, findings with confidence levels, metric baseline established. Nothing is designed this week.

**Week 22, Define and design.** Problem statement, opportunity tree, scope with an appetite, IA if relevant, full flow at mid fidelity, states matrix complete.

**Week 23, Build and test.** High fidelity, prototype, test with 5 users, fix what broke, gate passed, handoff written, instrumentation defined. Ship if Route A.

**Week 24, Measure and defend.** Result reported, the honest reading of it, the retrospective, the portfolio case study, and the 90 minute final defense.

## 5. The honest result requirement

This is the requirement that makes the sprint worth doing.

You will report what happened, not what you hoped. Specifically:

- The metric before and after, or the measurement plan and its first data if the window was too short
- Whether the change is distinguishable from noise, stated plainly
- What you would need to be confident
- If it did not work: why you think not, and which of your assumptions was wrong

**A negative result honestly reported and correctly reasoned scores higher than a positive result with no measurement.** Everyone claims their design worked. Almost nobody can show it. The ability to read a number against your own work, without flinching, is the rarest thing in this profession and it is what the final defense is looking for.

## 6. The final defense, 90 minutes

Seven rounds. The six standard ones, extended, plus a panel.

| Round | Time | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | 10 min | Your own project, plus anything from the full 24 weeks |
| Reasoning | 15 min | Five decisions across the whole program, not just this sprint |
| Live critique | 15 min | Two unseen surfaces, one of which is deliberately good |
| Adversarial | 15 min | Your capstone's weakest decision, attacked by two people |
| Handoff | 10 min | An engineer and a system consumer, together |
| Panel | 15 min | External guests. Whatever they ask. |
| Reflection | 10 min | The 24 weeks. What you were wrong about in week 1. |

**The panel round is the one that cannot be prepared for**, which is the point. External guests who have not read your work and owe you nothing.

## 7. What the portfolio case study must contain

Written in week 24, and it is the artefact you leave with.

1. The problem, framed from evidence
2. What you did and why, including what you rejected
3. The decision that was hardest, with the reasoning intact
4. The evidence, with participant counts and confidence
5. What you shipped
6. What happened, honestly
7. What you got wrong
8. What you would do next

Point 7 is the one that gets a designer hired, and it is the one nobody includes. A case study with no mistakes in it reads as a case study written by someone who did not look.

## 8. The last injection

Week 23. The measured result contradicts your design hypothesis. Whether that happens naturally or is injected, you will write a recommendation: ship it anyway, roll it back, or hold and gather more. With reasoning, and with the number that would change your recommendation.

## 9. What ends here and what does not

At week 24 the program ends. What continues:

- The design system, which the next cohort inherits along with your governance model
- The fundamentals repository, which has 24 weeks of why sessions, teardowns, and explainers in it
- Your ledger, which is what any reference or placement conversation is built from
- The habit, if it took: nothing is done until someone else can build it, and no decision is real until you can defend it to someone who wants it to be wrong

## 10. Reading

Nothing new is assigned. Week 21 is a re-read of your own week 1 work, all of it, and one written page on what you now see in it that you could not see then.
