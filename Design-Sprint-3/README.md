# Design Sprint 3: Product Thinking and the End to End Feature

**Weeks 9 to 12. UX1 to UX5.**

The project: a **B2B logistics platform for small transporters**. Fleet owners with 3 to 40 trucks, currently running the business on WhatsApp, a diary, and phone calls. The company has 400 paying customers and cannot get them past the first month.

This is the sprint where design meets money. Every decision now has a business consequence and you will be asked to state it.

## What ships by week 12

- The activation problem defined with numbers, not adjectives
- An information architecture validated by tree test, not by opinion
- One complete feature designed end to end: entry, empty, first success, repeat use, failure, recovery
- A prototype in ProtoPie or Figma with real logic, tested with 5 users
- The metric definition, the instrumentation plan, and the experiment that would prove the design worked
- A written scope decision: what you cut and why, in Shape Up terms
- A persuasion audit of your own design against the dark pattern catalogue

## The new pressure

Sprints 1 and 2 had a government client, so the business tension was mild. This client has revenue, churn, and a runway. You will be asked, at the defense, what your design does to the money. "It improves the experience" is not an answer.

## The line you will be pushed across

In week 11 you will be asked to add something that increases a number and costs the user something. What you do, and how you argue it, goes in the ledger. There is no correct answer that is simply "refuse". There is a correct way to reason about it.
