# Day 1

## LEARN — 75 minutes

| Source | What exactly | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **`06-ethics-position.md`** | The full framework | 20 min |
| **AIGA** or **IxDA** | Any published professional code of conduct for designers | 20 min |
| **ACM Code of Ethics** | Sections 1 and 2. Written for computing, and more concrete than most design codes. | 35 min |

**What to notice:** most design codes of ethics are vague enough to be useless in a meeting. The ACM's is more specific because computing has had to defend itself in court. Yours should be closer to the ACM's than to the vague ones — usable, not aspirational.

→ **Commit** `students/UX{n}/week12/session1-learning.md`

## DO — 2 hours

### E2.1 — Your ethics position

**Method**
Write it. Five sections.

1. **What you will not do.** Specific practices, named. Not "I will not deceive users" — name the patterns.
2. **What you will do under protest**, and what "under protest" means concretely. Usually: I build it, and I put the objection in writing in the decision record.
3. **What you consider acceptable persuasion**, with your test from E1.2.
4. **How you would raise a concern.** The actual escalation path: who you tell, in what order, in writing or verbally, and what you do if nothing changes.
5. **What you would need in order to refuse.** Honest. Savings, seniority, a written policy, a union, another offer. This section is where most positions become believable or stop being believable.

**Worked example** — section 1:

> **What I will not do:**
> - I will not build a consent control that is pre-checked, or one where the accept and decline paths have unequal effort.
> - I will not build a countdown or scarcity indicator for a limit that is not real and verifiable.
> - I will not build confirmshaming — an opt-out label whose text is designed to make the user feel bad rather than to describe the action.
> - I will not build a cancellation path with more required steps than the corresponding sign-up path.
> - I will not remove a visible focus indicator, or ship a flow whose primary path is impossible by keyboard.
>
> Each of these is a thing I can point at on a screen. That is deliberate. A position I cannot apply to a specific Figma frame in a specific meeting is decoration.

**Done when**
- [ ] All five sections
- [ ] Section 1 names specific practices, each pointable-at on a screen
- [ ] "Under protest" is defined as a concrete action
- [ ] The escalation path is a real sequence with named roles
- [ ] Section 5 is honest and specific, not heroic

## DO — 2 hours

### E2.2 — The written refusal

**Method**
The scenario: your manager has asked you to ship the confirmshaming and the fake timer from E1.3. Write the message you send.

Requirements:
1. **State what you will not do, and be specific.** One or two things, not a list of grievances.
2. **Give the reason in terms your manager cares about.** Regulatory exposure, chargebacks, support load, reputational risk. Not "it is wrong".
3. **Offer the alternative you will build**, immediately, with an honest estimate of what it costs relative to what was asked.
4. **Name what you will do instead of nothing.** You are not refusing to work; you are refusing one specific thing.
5. **Say what happens if the decision goes against you.** Usually: I will build it, and I will record my objection in the decision log with my name on it, and I want the risk owner named.
6. **Keep it under 300 words.** A long refusal reads as a manifesto and gets forwarded around as one.

**Worked example**

> Subject: Cancellation flow — two changes I need to flag before I build
>
> I can have the retention offer and the streamlined flow ready before the week closes. Two of the five requested items I want to raise first.
>
> **The countdown timer.** As specified it resets on reload, so the "offer ends in 3 hours" is not a real limit. The FTC's dark patterns report and the CMA's choice architecture guidance both name fictional urgency as a deceptive practice, and there is enforcement history. I would rather not put a false claim in the interface. I will build the same offer without the timer, presented once, with real terms — my estimate is that it converts a few points lower.
>
> **The "No thanks, I don't care about saving money" decline label.** Same offer, different label: "No thanks". The guilt framing is what would get screenshotted, and support will absorb the complaints.
>
> Everything else in the spec I will build as written, including the retention offer, the pause option, and the win-back email.
>
> If you want the timer and the label as specified after reading this, I will build them, and I will record the decision and my objection in the decision log with the risk owner named. I would rather have that conversation now than after launch.
>
> Ready to start today either way.

Note the structure: it opens with what is being delivered, it isolates two specific items rather than resisting the whole brief, it gives external authority rather than personal preference, it offers a concrete alternative with an honest cost, it does not threaten, and it ends with a commitment to work.

**Done when**
- [ ] Under 300 words
- [ ] One or two specific refusals, not a list of complaints
- [ ] The reason is commercial or regulatory, not moral
- [ ] An alternative offered with an honest cost estimate
- [ ] What you will still build, stated
- [ ] What happens if you are overruled, stated
- [ ] It does not moralise and it does not threaten

**Why 4.1.3 matters here:** your flow is full of status changes — payment processing, plan changed, cancellation scheduled. A sighted user sees a banner appear. A screen reader user is told nothing unless you specified a live region. Most billing flows fail this.

→ **Commit** `session4-learning.md`

## DO — 2 hours (plus critique)

### C12.2 — Accessibility pass

**Method**
1. Keyboard: tab through the whole flow. Every action reachable, focus visible everywhere, focus order matching the visual order.
2. Modals: focus moves in on open, is trapped while open, Escape closes, focus returns to the trigger on close. Specify all four.
3. Status changes: every one gets a live region, and you specify polite or assertive and why.
4. Contrast: everything measured, including the disabled states and the focus rings.
5. Motion: reduced-motion variants all present.
6. Timing: if you kept any timed element, WCAG 2.2.1 applies — it must be adjustable, extendable, or turn-off-able. Note that your own ethics position may have already removed the only timer, which is worth pointing out in the defense.

**Done when**
- [ ] Full keyboard walkthrough documented, not asserted
- [ ] All four modal behaviours specified
- [ ] Every status change has a live region with politeness stated and justified
- [ ] All contrast measured with values recorded
- [ ] Reduced motion complete
- [ ] 2.2.1 addressed for anything timed

→ `students/UX{n}/week12/C12-2-a11y-pass.md`

# Day 5

## Defense — 60 minutes

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Interaction walk | 10 min | The state machine and three transitions, from memory |
| Motion defense | 10 min | Three animations: duration, easing, purpose, and reduced-motion variant |
| Dark pattern | 10 min | What you built, its mechanism, and its cost |
| Ethics position | 10 min | Where your line is and why it is there |
| The pressure | 10 min | You are pushed to ship the dark version. Hold or fold, and justify it. |
| Alternative | 10 min | A constraint card is drawn |

**On the pressure section.** Both failure modes are graded down. Collapsing immediately — "okay, I'd ship it" with no attempt to negotiate — shows you have no position. Grandstanding — "I would quit on the spot" — is not credible from a junior designer with no savings, and an examiner will say so. The pass is a real negotiation: what you concede, what you hold, what you escalate, and what you put in writing.

**Sprint 3 constraint cards:** all animation is banned by the platform team · the business requires the dark version and you have no leverage · a WCAG audit lands mid-sprint and blocks release · the payment provider's API forces a 6-second wait on cancellation · legal requires a 3-screen consent flow you did not want.

## C12.3 — Showpiece pass, 90 minutes

This flow is a consumer product with real visual range. It is the project that goes on the front of your portfolio, so it is held to a visual standard above "correct".

**Method**
1. Choose three hero screens from the shipped flow, for example the plan chooser, a lesson-complete moment, and the pause confirmation.
2. Raise the craft on those three: a documented type pairing decision, an imagery or illustration approach you have the right to use, deliberate use of colour, depth and spacing (Refactoring UI ch. 6 and ch. 8), and one signature transition polished to the motion spec.
3. Produce the same three screens in a dark theme through your tokens, not by hand.
4. Export at 2x. Record a 20-second prototype clip. Write a 100-word caption that states the problem, the decision, and the result you can claim.
5. Check the three screens still pass contrast and still have a reduced-motion version.

**Done when**
- [ ] Three hero screens at 2x, light and dark, built from tokens
- [ ] A type pairing decision with a reason
- [ ] A 20-second prototype clip
- [ ] A 100-word caption that makes no claim you cannot support
- [ ] Contrast and reduced-motion checks still pass

→ `students/UX{n}/week12/C12-3-showpiece.md`
