# Sprint 3 — Read First

**Weeks 9 to 12 · Interaction, motion, metrics and ethics**

---

## What changes from Sprint 2

Sprint 2 was about finding out what is true. Sprint 3 is about what happens between states — the transitions, the feedback, the microcopy, the moment a thing is deleted and cannot be recovered.

It is also the ethics sprint. You will be asked to build something manipulative, on purpose, and then to refuse a version of it and write down where your line is. That refusal is a graded deliverable.

---

## The project

**Sutra**, a fictional spoken-language learning app, and its subscription flow: signing up, changing a plan, pausing, cancelling and coming back. The client brief is "reduce churn", which is a goal, not a brief; you will define activation and the metric set before you design for it.

It is also the program's one consumer, visually ambitious project, and the front page of your portfolio (see the showpiece pass in week 12). It is chosen deliberately. Cancellation flows are where almost every company in the world behaves badly, so it is the best place to learn the difference between persuasion and manipulation while the stakes are only a design exercise.

## The books

**About Face** (Cooper) chapters on interaction and postures, for weeks 9 and 10.

**Google HEART paper** and the funnel chapters of **Lean Analytics** for week 9.

**Designing Interface Animation** (Val Head) for week 10. This is the one you actually work through.

**Ruined by Design** (Mike Monteiro) for week 11 into 12. Short, angry, and the point.

Also: **deceptive.design** (formerly darkpatterns.org) and the CMA and FTC published guidance on dark patterns. Real regulators, real enforcement actions, real fines. Read them, because "it is just design" stops being true when the fine arrives.

---

## The hard part

**Week 11 asks you to build a dark pattern.** A real one, competently made. You will design a cancellation flow that maximises retention using confirmshaming, a hidden exit, a fake urgency timer, and a pre-checked box.

Then you write down what it costs the user, in specific terms, and what you would say if your manager asked you to ship it.

**Week 12 asks you to refuse one.** Not all of them — one specific request, with a written reason, an alternative you would build instead, and an honest account of what refusing costs you professionally.

The graded thing is not whether you refuse. It is whether your line is somewhere defensible and whether you can say why it is there.

## What ships at the end of week 12

- A state machine for the subscription lifecycle, with every transition and every guard
- A funnel, an activation definition and a metric set with a guardrail, instrumented in the spec
- A latency and feedback plan tied to actual response-time thresholds
- A motion spec with duration, easing, and a purpose for every animation, plus reduced-motion alternatives
- A microcopy set for every state change
- A dark pattern audit of three real products, with the mechanism named
- One built dark pattern, with its user cost documented
- An ethics position with your line stated
- One written refusal, with an alternative
- The shipped flow, accessible and specified
- A showpiece pass: three hero screens, light and dark, built from tokens
- A portfolio checkpoint: live portfolio with two case studies, and your first mock interview (JR3)
- A post mortem
- 20 learning summaries
- A passed defense
