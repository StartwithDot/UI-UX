# Design Sprint 3, Admin

Private. General machinery is in `../Design-Sprint-1-Admin/`. This covers Sprint 3 only.

---

## What this sprint is testing

Whether a designer can reason about money without abandoning the user, and whether they can hold a line under commercial pressure with an alternative rather than a refusal.

## Weekly focus

**Week 9.** The activation definition is the whole week. The brief gives you the answer if you read it: accounts recording 10 or more trips in month one retain at four times the rate. A designer who defines activation as "completed signup" has read the funnel and not the retention data.

Watch for HEART tables with all five rows filled and three of them invented. An honest "we cannot measure happiness with what this company has" is stronger than a fabricated NPS row.

**Week 10.** The Shape Up appetite is where designers discover their scope instinct. Six weeks, four engineers. Most will overshoot by a factor of two and not know it.

The question that lands: "which of these would you cut if you had four weeks instead of six." A designer who cannot rank their own scope has not scoped, they have listed.

The driver strategy in P10.3 is the sprint's real design problem and the answer that keeps appearing is "an app for drivers", which fails on a 40MB budget and prepaid data. The designers who get somewhere are the ones who consider WhatsApp, SMS, IVR, or the owner entering it, and who state what each costs in data quality.

**Week 11.** The ethics decision arrives Monday. Do not preview which of the four requests it will be.

What you are grading is not the answer, it is the structure of the reasoning: the number, the user cost, the regulatory position, the decision, the alternative. A refusal with no alternative scores the same as compliance with no argument, because both leave the client with the problem and neither is a design contribution.

**Week 12.** The business case and the experiment design are where the sprint either landed or did not. An experiment with no minimum detectable effect means they do not understand what a result is. A business case that does not state what happens to the runway if they are wrong means they have not taken the client's position seriously.

## The injection

`failure-injections/sprint3.md`. Week 11, alongside the ethics request, which makes this the heaviest week of the program.

The primary metric changes from activation to retention. Chosen because it exposes whether the design was reasoned from the metric or decorated afterwards. A designer whose flow was genuinely built around the activation definition will find that most of it still holds and can say precisely which parts do not. A designer who designed first and attached metrics later will find nothing to change, which is the finding.

## Defense notes for this sprint

**Run the adversarial round as the founder.** Fourteen months of runway, eighteen engineering weeks left, and this design wants six of them.

> "Convince me this is worth a third of my remaining engineering capacity. And before you answer, my last designer told me their work would improve retention and it did nothing."

**What to listen for:** whether they can talk about money without either flinching from it or abandoning the user for it. Both failures are common and they look different. The flincher retreats to "good experience". The abandoner agrees to everything and produces the dark pattern.

**The ethics question in the adversarial round, for anyone who refused cleanly:** "so you have told me no, my activation problem is unchanged, and I have fourteen months. What am I supposed to do." A designer who has an alternative ready is doing the job. A designer who only has a principle is not yet useful to a company that has to survive.

## The failure specific to this sprint

Designers who treat business constraints as an imposition rather than as part of the problem. It shows up as a cut list with nothing on it, or a business case that assumes the design will work.

The other failure, less common but worth watching for, is the designer who enjoys the commercial framing so much that the persuasion audit comes back empty. Nobody's design has zero persuasive elements. An empty audit means they stopped looking at the point where looking became uncomfortable.
