# Design Sprint 6, Admin

Private. General machinery is in `../Design-Sprint-1-Admin/`. This covers the capstone and the close of the program.

---

## What this sprint is testing

Whether they can read a number against their own work without flinching.

Everything else has been examined across five sprints. This one examines honesty under the specific pressure of having spent four weeks on something that might not have worked.

## Before week 21

**Approve every capstone scope, in writing, before any research starts.** This is the most important admin action of the sprint.

The failure mode is universal: designers choose something too large. A four week project that cannot ship produces no number, and no number means the sprint's central requirement cannot be met. Push every scope down at least once. If it still sounds ambitious, push again.

Route A checks, all four or they are on Route B:
1. A named contact who has agreed in writing, not verbally
2. Five real users reachable
3. Something that can ship, even partially, inside four weeks
4. A number that exists before they start

Point 4 is the one that gets waived under pressure and it must not be. Without a baseline there is no result, and without a result this is Sprint 3 again with a different client.

**Book the external panel in week 20.** Three people if possible, two minimum. They must not have read the work and must not know the cohort. Brief them on one thing only: ask what you would actually ask, and do not be kind.

## Weekly focus

**Week 21.** Nothing is designed. Watch for anyone who opens Figma. After 20 weeks, a designer who still starts with screens has learned the process but not the instinct, and it goes in the ledger as the honest final assessment.

P21.2, the week 1 re-read, produces the most interesting writing of the program. Read all five. They will name things in their own week 1 work that you saw at the time and did not say. That is what the 24 weeks were for.

**Week 22.** Metrics committed before any screen exists. Check the commit timestamps, not the file contents. Metrics written after the design were chosen to flatter it, and the git history says which happened.

**Week 23.** Ship day for Route A. Whatever went live, went live. Partial is fine, and a designer who shipped one flow honestly has more to defend than one who shipped nothing beautifully.

The injection lands here for anyone whose result does not naturally contradict their hypothesis. For everyone else, reality has already done it.

**Week 24.** The result, the retrospective, the case study, and the final defense.

Read the results before the defenses. You need to know who got a null result, because those are the defenses that will either be the best or the worst of the program, and there is very little in between.

## Grading the result

This is the part that requires discipline from the admin.

| Result | Reporting | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Measured, sample stated, noise addressed | Strong |
| Negative | Measured, honest, the wrong assumption named | **Stronger** |
| Null | Measured, honest, states what would be needed for confidence | Strong |
| Positive | No measurement, "users love it" | Weak, and say so plainly |
| Positive | Sample too small to distinguish from noise, presented as a win | Weakest. This is the one to be hard on. |

**The last row is the important one.** A designer who presents an indistinguishable result as a success has learned to produce the appearance of rigour, which is worse than having no rigour, because it is harder to detect and it will follow them into their career.

## The final defense

90 minutes, seven rounds. Structure in the brief. The panel round is 15 minutes of external questions and you do not intervene, even when a guest asks something unfair. Unfair questions are the ones that happen in real rooms.

**The reflection round is the last thing in the program**, and it is the one to give the full ten minutes to.

The question: "what were you wrong about in week 1."

A designer who has been paying attention for 24 weeks has a long answer to that, and it is specific. A designer who says "I have learned so much" has been present but not looking, and after 24 weeks that is worth saying to them directly, kindly, and once.

## The close

**Sunday of week 24, per designer:**
1. Final ledger entry, including the Sprint 6 trajectory call
2. The honest conversation: their strongest capability, their real gap, and what a hiring manager will find
3. What you would say in a reference, read to them, so they know

**Cohort level:**
1. Design system handover written, so the next cohort inherits it with its governance intact
2. Program retro in `guide/decision-log.md`: which sprint produced the least learning per hour, which gate never worked, which injection did not land, and what you fixed for them that you should not have
3. The task cuts for the next cohort, decided while it is still fresh

## The failure specific to this sprint

Scope. Every time, in every cohort. It is the one thing that makes the difference between a capstone with a measured result and a capstone with a rationalisation.

The second failure is quieter and worth watching for in week 24: a designer who did good work, got a null result, and writes it up defensively, explaining it away rather than reading it. The whole program was pointed at that moment. If they flinch there, the honest ledger entry says so, because it is the thing that will most limit them in five years.
