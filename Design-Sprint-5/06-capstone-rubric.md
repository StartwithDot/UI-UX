# Sprint 5 Capstone Rubric

Exactly how weeks 17 to 20 are graded. Read it in week 17, not in week 20.

---

## How it works

Nine criteria, each scored 1 to 4 by the admin and one external reviewer separately. The two scores are compared; if they differ by more than one point, they talk until they agree or record the disagreement.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **4** | A hiring manager would be impressed and could not easily pick it apart |
| **3** | Solid. Would pass a real review with a few notes |
| **2** | Has the right pieces, but a key one is missing or unsupported |
| **1** | Absent, or present only in form |

**To pass the capstone you need:** no score of 1 on any criterion, at least four criteria at 3 or above, and both gates cleared (the evidence gate in weeks 17 and 19, the craft gate in week 18).

The defense (week 20) is scored separately with the standard rubric and can move a criterion by one point in either direction.

---

## The nine criteria

### 1. Choice and scope
| 4 | The project fits four weeks, the cut list is written down, and the choice is argued against at least one other option |
|---|---|
| 2 | A reasonable project, but the scope is vague or two weeks too big |
| 1 | Cannot say what was cut or why |

### 2. Evidence
| 4 | Real participants in weeks 17 and 19, findings carry confidence levels, and at least one finding contradicted the designer's own hypothesis |
|---|---|
| 2 | Research was done but findings are opinions, or only one round |
| 1 | Invented users, or findings that cannot be traced to a participant |

### 3. Problem framing and metrics
| 4 | Problem statement has no solution words; primary, input and guardrail metrics are defined before design; guardrail protects against a real harm |
|---|---|
| 2 | A good problem statement but metrics defined after the design |
| 1 | The metric is "user satisfaction" with no way to measure it |

### 4. Craft
| 4 | All states, both widths, both themes, motion with a purpose; the design looks considered, not just correct |
|---|---|
| 2 | The happy path is polished; the states are described, not designed |
| 1 | Missing states or inconsistent use of the system |

### 5. Systems
| 4 | System extensions are documented, reviewed and contributed back; no local forks without a written reason |
|---|---|
| 2 | Uses the system but extensions are undocumented |
| 1 | Bypasses the system |

### 6. Accessibility
| 4 | WCAG 2.2 AA conformance report with evidence per criterion, a screen reader pass, and the specific accessibility challenge for the project handled head-on |
|---|---|
| 2 | A checklist with ticks but little measured evidence |
| 1 | The accessibility gate was not run |

### 7. Ethics
| 4 | The ethical tension named in the proposal appears in the design decisions, and the designer says what it cost |
|---|---|
| 2 | An ethics section that could belong to any project |
| 1 | No ethics review |

### 8. Handoff and decisions
| 4 | An engineer could build from the spec without asking; the decision log shows decisions written at the time, not reconstructed |
|---|---|
| 2 | A spec with gaps; decision log reconstructed at the end |
| 1 | No spec, or no decision log |

### 9. The injection and honesty
| 4 | The post mortem names an earlier decision that made the injection cheaper or costlier, and the designer states what they still do not know |
|---|---|
| 2 | The post mortem describes what changed but blames the input |
| 1 | No post mortem |

---

## What does not earn points

- Number of screens
- Polish on screens that have no evidence behind them
- A long presentation. Twenty minutes means twenty minutes.
- Defending a decision with "users will like it"
