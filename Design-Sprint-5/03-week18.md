## The standards that apply, without being restated

Every standard from Sprints 1 to 4 is live this week. Nobody will remind you. The ones people drop first, in order:

1. Real content instead of lorem
2. Empty, loading, and error states
3. Contrast measured rather than assumed
4. Decision records written at the time rather than reconstructed on Friday
5. Focus indicators on custom components

**If you catch yourself about to drop one, log it in your decision record as a deliberate choice with a reason.** A logged shortcut is a decision. An unlogged one is a failure.

# Monday

## LEARN — 75 minutes, self-directed
Whatever your information architecture problem requires. If your project has a complex taxonomy, that is card sorting and IA. If it has a difficult flow, that is task analysis.

→ **Commit** `students/UX{n}/week18/day1-learning.md`

## DO — 3 hours

### K2.1 — Structure
Your information architecture. Justified by your research, not by convention. If you have a hierarchy, say why it is that hierarchy and what you rejected.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-1-structure.md`

### K2.2 — Flows
Every flow the scope includes. Happy path, and the failure paths. **The failure paths are where the design work is** — a booking flow that only handles successful bookings is not a booking flow, and your week 17 research will have told you that the failures are the whole problem.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-2-flows.md`

# Tuesday

## LEARN — 60 minutes, self-directed
Whatever your hardest screen needs. Usually a pattern you have not designed before — a complex form, a data-dense list, a multi-step wizard.

→ **Commit** `day2-learning.md`

## DO — 4 hours

### K2.3 — Mid-fidelity, everything

**Method**
1. Every screen in your scope, mid-fidelity, using the system.
2. Real content from the start. Real names, real amounts, real dates, real edge cases from your research — including the person whose name is 34 characters long and the appointment that was cancelled twice.
3. **Two decision records minimum, written today.** Not reconstructed on Friday.

**Done when**
- [ ] Every screen in scope exists
- [ ] Real content throughout, including the edge cases research surfaced
- [ ] System components used
- [ ] Two decision records, dated today

→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-3-midfi.md`

# Wednesday

## LEARN — 60 minutes, self-directed
Whatever you are weakest at. Be honest — your four reflections have told you what it is, and you have probably avoided it for sixteen weeks.

→ **Commit** `day3-learning.md`

## DO — 4 hours

### K2.4 — Every state
Empty, loading, partial, error, success, offline, and permission-denied for every screen where each is possible. The Sprint 1 standard.

**For your project specifically:** if connectivity is a constraint, offline is not an edge case, it is a primary state. If your users are on shared devices, the permission and privacy states are primary too.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-4-states.md`

### K2.5 — Content
Every string. Labels, hints, errors, empty states, confirmations, and notifications. Read it aloud once — the sentence you stumble over is the one to rewrite.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-5-content.md`

# Thursday

## LEARN — 60 minutes, self-directed
The accessibility requirements for your specific patterns. Read the APG page for whatever you actually built, not a general article.

→ **Commit** `day4-learning.md`

## DO — 4 hours (plus critique)

### K2.6 — High fidelity
The critical screens at full fidelity, both themes, every interactive state. Not everything needs to be high fidelity — decide which screens do, and say why those.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-6-hifi.md`

### K2.7 — Motion
Only what earns its place. The Sprint 3 standard: duration, easing, purpose, and reduced-motion variant per animation.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-7-motion.md`

### Thursday critique — 90 minutes
Present the whole flow. The critique focus: **which standard have you already quietly dropped?** Your peers will find it. It is easier if you name it first.

# Friday

## DO — 4 hours

### K2.8 — Accessibility pass
Full WCAG 2.2 AA self-audit. Every criterion, marked pass, fail, or not applicable, with evidence for each pass and a fix plan with a date for each fail.
→ `students/UX{n}/week18/K2-8-a11y.md`

## Craft gate — Friday afternoon

**What is checked:**
- Every screen in scope exists
- Every state designed
- Real content throughout
- Contrast measured, values recorded
- Keyboard path complete
- System components used, extensions contributed back through governance
- Decision records dated at the time, not backfilled

**If it fails,** week 19 absorbs the remediation, which means less time for testing, which weakens week 19's evidence gate. The gates compound deliberately.
