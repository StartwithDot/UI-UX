# the start of the week

## DO — 5 hours

### K4.1 — Finalise

**Method**
1. Every screen final. Both themes. Every state.
2. Every change from week 19 incorporated.
3. Every response to the injection incorporated.
4. **The consistency pass.** Go through everything once looking only for inconsistency: two different empty state treatments, two spacings for the same relationship, two words for the same concept. There will be some. There always are.
5. Nothing labelled "TODO" or "TBD" survives into a shipped file. Either resolve it or move it to the open questions in the spec, where it is an honest statement rather than an unfinished one.

**Done when**
- [ ] Every screen final in both themes
- [ ] Week 19 changes and injection responses all in
- [ ] Consistency pass done, with the fixes logged
- [ ] Zero TODOs in the design files
- [ ] Prototype walkable end to end including failure paths

→ `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-1-final.md`

# early in the week

## DO — 5 hours

### K4.2 — Handoff spec

The complete specification. Every section from Sprint 2's S8.1 and Sprint 4's documentation standard: layout with breakpoints, components with their source, every state, every interaction, motion, content, validation rules with exact messages, accessibility requirements, analytics events, and the open questions.

**The test:** an engineer who has never spoken to you could build this. Everything they would have to ask you is a gap.

→ `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-2-spec/`

### K4.3 — Decision log

Every significant decision across the four weeks, in one document. For each: what you decided, when, what you considered, why you chose it, what it cost, and whether you would make it again.

**The last column is the point.** A decision log where you would make every decision again is a decision log you have not thought about honestly.

→ `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-3-decisions.md`

# mid week

## DO — 4 hours

### K4.4 — Accessibility conformance report

**Method**
1. Every WCAG 2.2 AA success criterion. Pass, fail, or not applicable.
2. **Evidence for every pass.** A contrast measurement, a keyboard walkthrough note, a specified announcement. Not an assertion.
3. For every fail: the severity, who it affects, the fix, and the date.
4. For every "not applicable": why it does not apply. This is where people hide failures, so it is checked.
5. State what you could not verify in Figma and would need a build to confirm. Being clear about the limits of a design-stage audit is more credible than claiming full conformance from a prototype.

**Done when**
- [ ] Every AA criterion addressed
- [ ] Evidence attached to every pass
- [ ] Every fail has severity, impact, fix, and date
- [ ] Every N/A justified
- [ ] The limits of design-stage verification stated honestly

→ `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-4-conformance.md`

# late in the week

## DO — 4 hours (plus rehearsal)

### K4.5 — Presentation

**Method**
Twenty minutes. Built for someone who has never seen the project.

The structure that works:
1. **The problem** — 3 min. With evidence. Not with your process.
2. **What you found** — 4 min. The finding that changed your direction.
3. **The design** — 8 min. Walked, not described. Show the failure states, not only the happy path.
4. **What testing changed** — 3 min. One specific before and after.
5. **What you would do next** — 2 min. Including what is still wrong with it.

**The rules:**
- No process diagrams. Nobody outside design cares about your double diamond.
- Every claim about users has evidence behind it.
- Show the thing that did not work. A presentation with no failures reads as dishonest to anyone experienced.
- Rehearse it out loud, timed. Twenty minutes is shorter than you think.

**Done when**
- [ ] Twenty minutes, timed and rehearsed out loud
- [ ] Opens with the problem, not the process
- [ ] Every user claim traceable to evidence
- [ ] Failure states shown
- [ ] At least one thing that did not work, presented as such
- [ ] Ends with what is still wrong

→ `students/UX{n}/week20/K4-5-presentation/`

### Rehearsal critique — 90 minutes
Present to peers. They ask the hardest questions they can. Every question you fumble is a slide to fix tonight.

# the end of the week

## Presentation — 20 minutes plus questions

To an external reviewer. Someone who has not seen the project and does not know the program.

**Assessed on:** whether the problem is clear in the first three minutes · whether the design is shown rather than described · whether claims have evidence · whether the answers to questions are honest · whether they would hire you off this.

## Defense — 75 minutes

The whole program is in scope.

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and evidence | 10 min | The problem, the research, the confidence levels |
| Design walk | 15 min | Every screen, every state, from memory |
| Craft | 10 min | Spacing, type, colour, contrast — with numbers |
| Accessibility | 10 min | The conformance report, and the fails |
| Systems | 10 min | Your tokens, your components, your governance |
| Ethics | 5 min | Your position, and the problem in your own design |
| Cross-sprint | 10 min | Anything from weeks 1 to 20 |
| Alternative | 5 min | A constraint card |

**The cross-sprint section is where the program is actually tested.** It might be a Sprint 1 contrast question, a Sprint 2 sampling question, a Sprint 3 easing question, or a Sprint 4 versioning question. There is no revision list, because the point is whether it stuck.

**Sprint 5 constraint cards:** the primary user group is removed from scope · the budget is halved and you must cut half the scope · the platform changes from mobile to SMS-only · a legal requirement adds a mandatory consent step to your core flow · you must hand this over tomorrow and are unavailable afterwards.

## What is left

Sprint 6 is not more design work. It is turning twenty weeks of evidence into something that gets you hired: case studies, a portfolio, interview preparation, and a whiteboard practice regime.

You now have five projects with documented reasoning, real research, and defended decisions. Most people applying for junior design roles have three projects with no reasoning at all. Sprint 6 is about making sure yours reads that way.
