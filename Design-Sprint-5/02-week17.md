## About the LEARN block from here on

You still commit a learning summary for every session. The format changes:

1. **What I learned** — the source, and specifically what part
2. **Why I needed it this week** — tied to a task you are doing now
3. **What I did with it** — the specific decision it changed
4. **What I chose not to learn** — and why it waits

Point 4 is the new one, and it is the point of the exercise. In a four-week project with a fixed deadline, choosing what to remain ignorant of is a real skill.

# Day 1

## LEARN — 90 minutes, self-directed

Whatever your project needs before you can write a brief. For a health system that might be how appointment scheduling actually works in a district hospital. For the gig worker tool it might be the platform's actual payment terms. For the apprenticeship service it might be the eligibility rules.

**Not design theory.** You have four sprints of design theory. This week is domain knowledge, because you cannot frame a problem in a domain you do not understand.

→ **Commit** `students/UX{n}/week17/session1-learning.md`

## DO — 3 hours

### K1.1 — Your project brief

**Method**
Write the brief yourself. Sections: the context · who the users are and how you know · the problem as you currently understand it · the constraints, classified as fixed, technical, or assumed · what is explicitly out of scope · what you do not yet know.

**The out-of-scope section is graded.** A four-week project that does not exclude anything is a four-week project that will not finish. Name what you are not doing and why it is the right thing to cut.

**Done when**
- [ ] All six sections
- [ ] Users described with a source for the description
- [ ] Constraints classified into the three types
- [ ] Out of scope is specific and justified, not vague
- [ ] What you do not know is honest and listed

→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-1-brief.md`

### K1.2 — Your scoped task list

**Method**
1. Write every task for the four weeks. Your own IDs, your own structure.
2. For each: what it is, when it happens, what it depends on, and what "done" means.
3. **Mark the critical path.** If one task slips, which others slip with it?
4. Mark what you cut. Then mark what you would cut next, if you lost time.
5. Total the hours. If it exceeds 4 weeks × 5 days × 4 hours = 80 hours, you have over-scoped and you must cut now, not in week 20.

**Worked example** — the cut list, which is the part that matters:

> **Already cut, and why:**
> - The clinician-side interface. It is a real need and it is a second product. Including it would mean doing both badly.
> - Multi-language support beyond the copy deck. I will write the English copy with translation in mind and note the Devanagari line-height implications, but I will not produce a translated flow.
>
> **What I cut next if I lose three days,** in order:
> 1. The dark theme. It is a token re-point and it can be done in a day later. Nothing depends on it.
> 2. The reschedule flow's edge cases — same-day and after-hours. I keep the main reschedule path and document the edge cases as open questions in the spec.
> 3. The motion spec beyond the three critical transitions.
>
> **What I will not cut under any circumstances:** the accessibility conformance work, and the round of testing with real users. Cutting either invalidates everything else, because then I have no evidence and no proof it works for the people who most need it.

**Done when**
- [ ] Every task listed with a definition of done
- [ ] Dependencies mapped, critical path marked
- [ ] Total hours calculated and within budget
- [ ] A cut list, ordered
- [ ] A stated never-cut list with the reason

→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-2-task-list.md`

# Day 2

## LEARN — 75 minutes, self-directed
Whatever you need to choose your research methods well for *this* problem. Re-read the parts of Sprint 2 you have forgotten, and be honest about which those are.

→ **Commit** `session2-learning.md`

## DO — 2.5 hours

### K1.3 — Research plan

Sprint 2's eight-section standard: questions, methods with reasons, who, how many and why, the protocol, what would falsify each question, what you cannot learn this way, and the timeline.

Plus one section Sprint 2 did not require: **what you will do if recruitment fails.** You have four weeks and no slack. A plan that assumes recruitment works is not a plan.

# Day 3

## LEARN — 60 minutes, self-directed
Domain depth. The thing you discovered at the start of the week that you did not know enough about.

→ **Commit** `session3-learning.md`

## DO — 4 hours

### K1.4 — Run the research

**Method**
Minimum three real participants. Five is better. Consent to the Sprint 2 standard, anonymised, notes straight away.

**Contextual research counts and is often better here.** Watching someone actually use the current system, or watching the receptionist who *is* the current system, will teach you more in an hour than three interviews. If your project has an observable current state, observe it.

**Done when**
- [ ] Three or more participants, consented and anonymised
- [ ] Notes straight away
- [ ] At least one session was observation rather than only conversation, where that was possible
- [ ] "What surprised me" filled for each

→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-4-research.md`

# Day 4

## LEARN — 60 minutes, self-directed
Whatever your findings just exposed as a gap. This is the most honest LEARN block of the week, because the gap chose itself.

→ **Commit** `session4-learning.md`

## DO — 3 hours (plus critique)

### K1.5 — Synthesis
Findings with denominators, confidence levels, and at least one contradiction left unresolved. Sprint 2's standard, applied without being told to.
→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-5-findings.md`

### K1.6 — Problem statement
The Sprint 1 shape: `[user] cannot [job] because [obstacle], which costs [user cost] and [business cost].` Grounded in this week's evidence, with each element traced to a finding.
→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-6-problem.md`

### Critique — 90 minutes
Present your problem statement and your scope. Two critique questions: **is the problem the one your evidence supports?** and **will this scope finish in three more weeks?**

# Day 5

## DO — 3 hours

### K1.7 — Metrics and constraints
Primary, input, and guardrail metrics, each with a measurement method. Constraints classified, with the assumed ones flagged for challenge.
→ `students/UX{n}/week17/K1-7-metrics.md`

## Evidence gate — Day 5 afternoon

You present. Nothing proceeds to design until this passes.

**What is checked:**
- Real participants, properly consented and anonymised
- Findings with denominators and confidence levels
- A problem statement traceable to evidence
- Metrics with a guardrail
- A scope that is credible for three remaining weeks
- Assumed constraints identified

**If it fails,** you get the weekend and the start of the week. Design starts early in the week and week 18 is compressed. This is the real cost of a weak week 17 and it is not softened.
